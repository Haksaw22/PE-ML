## Audit 2 — The subsumption verdicts (Merhav–Feder, van Trees, Kolter, iDAD/ALINE, Willems)

**Verdict: WEAKENED** (confidence: medium)

I re-verified the five cited subsumption bodies of work against what the program actually needed them to prove. Verdicts 1 and 3 hold up as genuine, well-targeted citations (near-exact subsumption of the specific claims). Verdict 2 is plausible but rests on a general philosophical fact (prediction-without-identification is textbook) rather than a demonstrated formal correspondence to the program's specific δ quantity, so it is territorial-adjacency leaning toward exact rather than confirmed exact. Verdict 4 — the collective kill of RL wedges C1-C5, flagged for special scrutiny — is the clearest instance of over-trust: all three cited papers (Kolter 2011, Bhandari et al. 2018, Ghosh & Bellemare 2020) are TD stability/convergence-rate analyses under a *fixed* sampling distribution or representation; none of them propose or analyze active per-skill excitation scheduling (C1/C2), self-terminating per-mode exploration (C3), or excitation-aware replay curation (C4). The "same territory" (feature-covariance/λ_min conditioning of TD) was pattern-matched to "same object" (algorithms for actively achieving and exploiting that conditioning), which is not established by any of the three citations. This confirms the meta-check's suspicion specifically at verdict 4, while verdicts 1, 2, 3 survive scrutiny reasonably well.

**Key findings:**
- Verdict 1 (block-Hankel rank = McMillan degree = cyclic-module dimension, classical): CONFIRMED exact subsumption. Ho-Kalman realization theory (Ho & Kalman 1966) and N4SID (Van Overschee & De Moor) are textbook results establishing exactly this equivalence between Hankel-matrix rank, minimal state-space realization order, and cyclic-module dimension. Willems' fundamental lemma is a genuinely adjacent (not identical) result — it concerns rank conditions on input/output Hankel matrices from persistently-exciting data for behavioral/data-driven representability, not the realization-order identity itself — but citing it alongside Ho-Kalman/N4SID as supporting context is accurate, not a mis-citation. independent-mode-poe-note.md sec.2/7 correctly frames this as 'the tools are all mature.'
- Verdict 2 (delta/prediction-below-identification pre-empt subsumed by Merhav-Feder + van Trees): territorial match confirmed at the general-principle level. Merhav & Feder, 'Universal Prediction' (IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory 1998) is precisely the survey establishing that sequential/individual-sequence prediction can achieve near-optimal risk without the predictor ever converging to (identifying) the generating distribution -- a formal instance of prediction-without-identification. Van Trees' Bayesian CRB (Detection, Estimation and Modulation Theory, 1968) is the standard tool for separating achievable estimation risk from prediction risk under a prior. However, I could not find or reconstruct in the available notes the specific formal step mapping the program's delta quantity onto a Merhav-Feder/van-Trees theorem statement -- the correspondence is asserted, not derived on the record. Rate as (a)/(b) borderline: legitimate textbook generality, but the claimed subsumption is not verifiably an exact theorem match, only a sound conceptual one.
- Verdict 3 (active/closed-loop excitation subsumed by iDAD/DAD/ALINE + Bombois/Gevers): CONFIRMED as well-targeted, near-exact for the scope claimed. iDAD (Ivanova et al. 2021, NeurIPS -- 'Implicit Deep Adaptive Design: Policy-Based Experimental Design without Likelihoods') and ALINE (2025, NeurIPS -- 'Joint Amortization for Bayesian Inference and Active Data Acquisition') are real, on-point amortized/policy-based Bayesian experimental design frameworks. Bombois, Scorletti, Gevers et al.'s 'Least Costly Identification Experiment for Control' line (Automatica 2006 and follow-ups) is exactly the closed-loop, feedback-performance-preserving excitation-design literature the verdict credits it with. The verdict's own 'mostly subsumed' hedge correctly anticipates that neither line covers RL/skill-specific excitation, which is properly left to verdict 4.
- Verdict 4 (C1-C5 collectively subsumed by Kolter 2011 + Ghosh-Bellemare 2020 + Bhandari et al. 2018): NOT SUPPORTED at the generality claimed -- this is the load-bearing failure. Kolter 2011 ('The Fixed Points of Off-Policy TD') bounds the *quality/error of the TD fixed point* under a given off-policy sampling distribution (a stability/robustness guarantee for a fixed data distribution). Bhandari, Russo & Singal 2018 ('A Finite Time Analysis of TD Learning with Linear Function Approximation') gives finite-time *convergence-rate* bounds for TD, primarily on-policy (with a projected off-policy extension under further assumptions), with rate depending on lambda_min of the feature covariance under a *fixed* stationary/behavior distribution. Ghosh & Bellemare 2020 ('Representations for Stable Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning') characterizes which *state representations* keep off-policy TD stable. None of the three papers propose, analyze, or bound: (C1) task-weighted/per-mode excitation reward shaping, (C2) an active control law driving each skill-block toward lambda_min >= alpha_i, (C3) a self-terminating stopping rule per skill once a PE certificate is met, or (C4) excitation-aware replay-buffer curation. These are algorithmic exploration/curation questions the cited works simply do not address -- they answer 'given a fixed distribution/representation, does/how-fast does TD converge,' not 'how should an agent actively schedule, terminate, and curate exploration across skills using excitation certificates.' This is the pattern the meta-check warned about: 'same territory' (feature-covariance/lambda_min conditioning for TD stability) mislabeled as 'same object' (algorithms for actively achieving/exploiting that conditioning). The independent-mode-poe-note.md itself implicitly concedes this by keeping 'one algorithmic wedge... live' after the collective kill -- but the collective 'mostly subsumed' verdict for C1-C5 overclaims: at most the papers subsume a necessary-condition/backdrop fact (well-conditioned features stabilize/speed TD), not the active-scheduling, self-termination, or curation mechanisms.
- Verdict 5 (thin residual kept as instrument, epistemic status like scaling laws/NTK): not itself a subsumption claim to overturn -- it is a 'survives' call and is internally consistent with the verdict-2 finding (the delta pre-empt argument was narrowly killed, not all of A1).

**What should change:** Re-open verdict 4 specifically: split C1-C5 back into individually-gated claims rather than treating them as collectively killed. Recharacterize Kolter 2011 / Bhandari et al. 2018 / Ghosh-Bellemare 2020 as establishing only a necessary-condition backdrop (TD stability/convergence rate is governed by feature-covariance conditioning, i.e., an excitation-like quantity) -- not as subsuming the active algorithmic content of C1 (excitation-weighted reward shaping), C2 (per-mode excitation-driving control law), C3 (self-terminating per-mode exploration), or C4 (excitation-aware replay curation). These four should revert to un-gated/open status (as B3/B4 were flagged) rather than 'subsumed,' pending a real search for prior art specifically on active exploration scheduling and replay curation conditioned on excitation/PE certificates (e.g., look at curriculum RL, prioritized experience replay with information-gain criteria, and multi-task/skill-conditioned exploration bonus literature, none of which were checked here). Verdicts 1 and 3 can stand as-is. Verdict 2 should be flagged as 'plausible but formally unverified correspondence' rather than fully settled -- worth a follow-up check that the program's delta quantity is actually an instance of a Merhav-Feder or van-Trees bound rather than just thematically similar.

full-Gram and beats raw shot-count in every reported slice on a trained transformer — but it does not validate

**Key findings:**
